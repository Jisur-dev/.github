## The Problem

Every year, thousands of Computer Science graduates and self-taught beginners step into the software industry with strong theoretical concepts, yet almost no experience of what the job actually looks like. Many rely on regular courses and tutorial-driven learning, which create a false sense of progressteaching isolated syntax and guided solutions without exposure to complex, real-world development environments.

As a result, learners can solve isolated algorithm problems or follow step-by-step builds, but have never sat through a sprint planning meeting. They can write a function, but have never had it challenged in a real code review. They know what Git is, but have never resolved a high-stakes merge conflict under a real project deadline.

The result is a major gap that neither traditional academia, structured tutorials, nor standard bootcamps close: aspiring developers who are technically capable on paper, but professionally untested when stepping into real-world codebases.

## Our Idea

Jisur exists to close that gap not with more courses, but with a real job.

We built **The Bridge Program**: a 12-week simulated engineering organization where Computer Science students are treated as junior engineers from day one. They join daily stand-ups, work sprints, open real pull requests, review each other's code, respond to simulated incidents, and ship real features under real deadlines all while building deep, interview-grade fluency in the fundamentals that actually get tested in technical interviews.

The program ends the way a real onboarding period would: with interviews, mock and real, built entirely from the system participants spent three months designing, breaking, and fixing themselves.

## Our Vision

We want Jisur to become the standard bridge between a Computer Science degree and a first engineering role across the region the place employers trust to have already done the filtering they'd otherwise have to do themselves, and the place students trust to turn four months of effort into a real career outcome.

Concretely, that means:

- **For students:** a structured, mentored path from "I studied this" to "I can do this" with a real contribution history, real feedback, and real interview practice to show for it.
- **For employers:** a pipeline of junior engineers who already know how to work in a team, not just how to code reducing onboarding risk and ramp-up time.
- **For the ecosystem:** a public, transparent, evidence-based alternative to bootcamp hype one that measures itself honestly, cohort by cohort, and publishes what it finds.

## How It Works

| | Weekends | Weekdays |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Deep, multi-week mastery of interview-tested fundamentals: data structures & algorithms, SQL, system design, Git, testing, Agile practices | Full Agile simulation building Real World Projects: stand-ups, sprints, PRs, code review, incidents |
| **Format** | Concept + guided practice, then independent/timed practice and mock-interview drilling | Real tickets, real teammates, a real mentor and Tech Lead structure |
| **Ends in** | Mock and real technical interviews  | A handover package for the next cohort |

No slideshows. No toy exercises repeated from a textbook. A compressed, mentored version of the first four months of a professional software engineering job.

## Our Principles

- **Real over simulated wherever possible.** Real Git history, real product, real deadlines simulation is only used where reality genuinely can't be (e.g., a staged production incident).
- **Mentorship that fades on purpose.** Support is heaviest in week one and deliberately, structurally reduced every phase after because that's how real engineers actually grow.
- **The codebase outlives the cohort.** Everyone builds on someone else's work and leaves something for the next person.
